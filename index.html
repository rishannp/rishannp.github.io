A 100-year problem.
We solved it in two.

EEG electrodes work much as they did in the 1920s. They still struggle to reach the scalp through thick, curly or afro-textured hair, so people get turned away from clinics and quietly left out of research.

In one review, five of eighty-one EEG papers included any Black participants. I kept seeing it happen in my own experiments, and so did my co-founders, Peter Bryan and Merlin Angel Kelly.

Synaptive is the ethical healthcare company we started to fix it. Our first product, Syn-R1, is a passive, drop-in electrode that reaches the scalp first time, on every head. If that's why you're on this page, I'd like to hear from you.

Research is only useful
if it leaves the lab.

I did my PhD in UCL's Bioelectronics Group, building non-invasive BCIs that read motor intent in people with ALS.

Most of that work was about one stubborn problem: EEG signals drift from one day to the next, so a system that works on Monday falls over by Friday. I used graph neural networks and adaptive models to make BCIs that keep up with the person using them.

It was good science, and a lot of it stayed in papers. So I spent the next few years learning how research turns into something people can buy and use: Cambridge EnterpriseTECH, Neuroworks' What If, iCURe Engage, a Bio-Spark fellowship, and a year as an Impact Fellow at UCL's Institute of Healthcare Engineering. Synaptive came out of that.

I also co-founded the UK BCI Consortium, sit on the BCI Society's student and postdoc committee, and teach school students through the Brilliant Club and STEM Learning.

Tools I use most: PyTorch, PyTorch Geometric, TensorFlow, MNE, EEGLAB, pylsl, Python and MATLAB.
